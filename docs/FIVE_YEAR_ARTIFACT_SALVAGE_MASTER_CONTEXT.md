## WTF-stupid-simple truth

Randy did not spend the last several years building a pile of unrelated apps.

He repeatedly built pieces of the same larger system from different directions:

> A human-owned continuity system that lets AI absorb complexity, preserve truth, recover old work, act without constant babysitting, prove what it did, learn from failure, and survive changes in models, apps, machines, projects, and years.

The individual projects are experiments, products, organs, and proofs.

They are not the whole system.

The system underneath them has five recurring enemies:

1. Continuity tax — having to re-explain project state, authority, failures, constraints, and history whenever the model, chat, device, or tool changes.
2. Orchestration tax — having to tell the AI which skill, repo, tool, proof step, or expert frame to use for routine work.
3. Trust tax — AI saying done, working, current, or deployed when the real human path was not proven.
4. Waste tax — useful prior work, failed attempts, mechanisms, research, prompts, and artifacts being discarded or forgotten.
5. Complexity tax — the human being forced to understand technical machinery that the system should absorb.

The durable direction is:

> AI absorbs complexity. Human keeps authority. Nothing useful gets lost.

## Why this exists

The same failure kept recurring:

- important realizations happened in chat;
- the answer was useful;
- the conversation moved on;
- the insight was not promoted into a durable project artifact;
- later work had to rediscover the same truth.

This file exists to prevent that.

Future AI systems should read this as lineage and architecture context, not as permission to merge every project or to treat historical claims as current authority.

# The five taxes

## Continuity tax

Every model/chat/device/tool change risks forcing the human to restate:
- what project this is;
- what is authoritative;
- what already failed;
- what was proven;
- what constraints matter;
- where to resume.

Goal:

> The next intelligence inherits the competence earned by the previous one.

## Orchestration tax

The human should not repeatedly have to say:
- use Artifact Compass;
- now Salvage;
- inspect GitHub;
- check Drive;
- verify current authority;
- run proof;
- continue.

Goal:

> Harness automatically routes the right working method when intent is inferable.

## Trust tax

False finishes are expensive.

Goal:

> Claims cannot outrun evidence.

Permanent rule:

> Assertion is not evidence. Evidence is not proof. Proof is not certification. Certification cannot exceed proof.

## Waste tax

Old work repeatedly contains useful mechanisms.

Goal:

> Preserve computation, not clutter.

Use lineage, hashes, source packets, salvage cards, canonical status, historical status, and the weird ledger.

## Complexity tax

The human should not have to understand or conduct infrastructure to get useful work.

Goal:

> WTF-simple front, rigorous machinery behind it.

# The system is not more AI


# Constitution vs learned policy vs machinery

Four-layer model:

## Layer 1 — Constitution
Human-owned and hard to change.

Includes safety, privacy, authority, destructive-action boundaries, owner approval requirements, public/private boundaries.

AI may propose changes.

AI should not autonomously promote changes here.

## Layer 2 — Current truth
Project authority, live state, work orders, evidence.

Changes frequently.

Must have one canonical source per fact.

## Layer 3 — Learned policy
Candidate lessons and reusable rules.

Must be tested, versioned, reversible, conflict-aware, and retireable.

## Layer 4 — Deterministic machinery
Tests, validators, scripts, schemas, gates, and enforcement.

Preferred destination for stable repeated reasoning.
