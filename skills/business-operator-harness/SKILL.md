## Purpose

Make Harness Card useful for building, launching, proving, and operating businesses — not only software projects.

This skill exists for founders/operators who can describe the outcome they want but should not have to already know marketing, pricing, distribution, sales, analytics, operations, or growth terminology.

It does not replace the canonical reusable skills in the salvage suite. It orchestrates them.

## Default rule

Do not tell the owner to "do marketing" or "build an audience" as a generic answer.

Recover:
- who has the problem;
- how often it hurts;
- what they do now;
- what they already pay in money/time;
- what smallest offer solves a real part of it;
- how that buyer can discover/trust/buy it without requiring the owner to become a social-media personality;
- what can be automated;
- what evidence proves the business loop is working.

## Governing principle

**Demand -> Offer -> Proof -> Distribution -> Transaction -> Fulfillment -> Retention/Referral -> Learning**

Software completion is not business completion.

A working product with no discovery, transaction, or repeatable acquisition path is IMPLEMENTED_UNPROVEN as a business.

## Ten-pass business sweep

When the owner asks for a deep business pass, perform all ten unless clearly irrelevant.

### Pass 1 — Problem and buyer
Identify the painful job, buyer, urgency, frequency, existing workaround, and strongest evidence that the problem exists.

### Pass 2 — Current market
Inspect current alternatives, substitutes, DIY behavior, incumbent pricing, service pricing, free tools, and what buyers complain about.

### Pass 3 — Offer
Define the smallest valuable promise, exact deliverable, boundaries, time-to-value, proof artifact, and next logical paid step.

### Pass 4 — Pricing and economics
Model price, payment fees, infrastructure, support burden, refund/dispute exposure, acquisition cost ceiling, contribution margin, and whether a low-price offer is an entry wedge or viable standalone product.

### Pass 5 — Trust and conversion
Audit landing page clarity, evidence, examples, risk reversal, privacy, terms, refund/support path, checkout friction, credibility, and whether the user can understand the purchase without a sales call.

### Pass 6 — Distribution without fame
Prefer channels that do not require followers:
- search intent;
- product-led free utility;
- partner/referral distribution;
- useful directories/marketplaces where appropriate;
- permissioned or carefully targeted proof-first outreach;
- customer referrals;
- earned links/resources;
- integrations/embeds.

Do not default to "post on social media."

### Pass 7 — Acquisition automation
Map:
source -> qualify -> personalize/prove -> contact/discover -> visit -> free value -> capture -> purchase -> fulfillment -> follow-up -> rescan/renew/referral.

Automate stable steps. Keep legal/reputational owner gates.

### Pass 8 — Operations and reliability
Inspect payment authority, idempotency, fulfillment, delivery/recovery, support, refunds, abuse, privacy, logs, failure recovery, and owner interruption rate.

### Pass 9 — Measurement
Define the smallest useful funnel:
- qualified prospects/visitors;
- scan or activation starts;
- successful value event;
- optional contact capture;
- checkout starts;
- paid conversions;
- successful fulfillment;
- refunds/disputes;
- repeat use/rescan;
- referral/partner source.

Avoid vanity metrics unless tied to a decision.

### Pass 10 — Next experiment
Choose one bounded experiment with:
- hypothesis;
- audience;
- channel;
- offer;
- sample size/timebox;
- cost ceiling;
- success threshold;
- failure interpretation;
- next action.

Do not launch five channels at once.

## No-audience rule

If the owner has no followers, no social habit, or no personal brand, treat that as a design constraint — not a deficiency.

Build distribution around:
- high-intent search;
- direct product utility;
- credible proof;
- partners who already have the audience;
- referrals from successful users;
- highly targeted outreach where lawful and reputation-safe.

## Proof-first outreach rule

Generic mass "SEO/web/AI audit" outreach is a high-risk trust pattern.

When direct outreach is used:
- prove a specific observable issue first;
- be truthful about how the prospect was found;
- avoid fake personalization;
- minimize volume;
- provide immediate standalone value;
- make the commercial intent clear;
- comply with applicable marketing/email law and opt-out requirements;
- stop on opt-out;
- never fabricate results, urgency, rankings, or customer outcomes.

## Price-ladder rule

Do not assume one price must carry the whole business.

Classify each offer as one of:
- FREE ACQUISITION
- LOW-FRICTION PROOF PURCHASE
- CORE PRODUCT
- EXPANSION / IMPLEMENTATION
- RECURRING VALUE

If a low-ticket offer cannot support paid acquisition, treat it as a wedge unless evidence proves sufficient organic/partner volume.

## Owner interruption rule

Use Owner Interruption Minimizer.

The owner should normally be required only for:
- binding/legal decisions;
- spending above explicit authority;
- unusual refunds/disputes;
- high-reputation outreach changes;
- irreversible publication claims;
- material price/offer changes not covered by an approved experiment.

Repeated stable decisions should become policy.

## Evidence states

Use:
- IDEA
- DEMAND_SIGNAL
- OFFER_DEFINED
- TRANSACTION_READY
- FIRST_PAID_PROOF
- REPEATABLE_SALES_SIGNAL
- PROFITABLE_CHANNEL_SIGNAL
- OPERATING_LOOP_PROVEN
- BLOCKED
- RETIRED

Do not call a business proven because the website works.

## Closeout

A business work order closes with:
- current evidence state;
- what was learned;
- exact next experiment;
- cost/time ceiling;
- owner action required, if any;
- what the harness can execute without owner involvement.

## Success condition

This skill becomes PROVEN after it drives at least two distinct business/product workstreams from recovered context to measurable market experiments, with durable state, bounded owner interruption, and evidence-based next decisions.
